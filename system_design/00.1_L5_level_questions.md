For an L5 (Senior) candidate with your level of experience (11.5 years), the interviewers aren't just looking for a "working" diagram. They are looking for **trade-off fluency**—the ability to explain why one solution is better than another given specific constraints.

Here are the top 5 topics you must master to clear the L5 bar at Google or Amazon in 2026.

---

## 1. Data Modeling & Scaling (The "Storage" Pillar)

At L5, you can't just say "I'll use a database." You must justify the *type* of database and how it grows.

* **SQL vs. NoSQL:** Know exactly when to use Relational (ACID, complex joins) vs. NoSQL (Key-Value, Wide-column, Document) for scale.
* **Sharding & Partitioning:** Master **Consistent Hashing**. Explain how to handle "Hot Shards" (e.g., a celebrity on a social app) and how to choose a shard key that prevents data skew.
* **Indexing:** Be able to discuss the performance trade-offs of B-Trees vs. LSM Trees (Write-heavy vs. Read-heavy workloads).

## 2. Global Availability & Latency

Google and Amazon operate globally; your design must too.

* **Multi-Region Deployment:** Discuss Active-Active vs. Active-Passive setups.
* **CDNs & Edge Computing:** Explain how to use Content Delivery Networks not just for static images, but for "Edge Logic" (e.g., authentication at the nearest PoP).
* **Replication Lag:** If a user in India updates their profile, what happens if they immediately refresh? Discuss **Read-after-Write consistency** vs. **Eventual consistency**.

## 3. Resilience & "The Blast Radius"

A Senior Engineer is judged by how their system fails, not just how it works.

* **Circuit Breakers & Retries:** Explain how to prevent "Cascading Failures." If Service A is slow, how do you stop it from killing Service B? (Exponential backoff and Jitter).
* **Load Shedding:** How does your system decide which 10% of users to "drop" during a massive traffic spike to keep the other 90% alive?
* **Observability (SLIs/SLOs):** Move beyond "logging." Discuss how you monitor P99 latencies and set alerts that matter to the business.

## 4. Asynchronous Workflows (Decoupling)

In Big Tech, synchronous requests are "expensive." Decoupling is the key to scale.

* **Message Queues (Kafka/RabbitMQ):** When to use a queue for "Fire and Forget" vs. when a user needs a real-time response.
* **Idempotency:** This is a "must-have" for L5. If a network glitch causes a "Charge Credit Card" request to be sent twice, how does your system ensure the user is only charged once?
* **Event-Driven Architecture:** Discuss how different microservices communicate without being tightly coupled.

## 5. Security & Cost-Aware Design (The 2026 Standard)

Modern Senior interviews now explicitly look for "Architectural Maturity."

* **AuthN/AuthZ:** How do you handle identity at scale? (JWTs vs. Session Tokens, OAuth flows).
* **PII & Compliance:** How do you segregate sensitive user data (GDPR/CCPA) so it doesn't end up in your logs?
* **Cost Optimization:** In 2026, "unlimited scale" is a red flag. Mention **storage tiering** (moving old data to S3 Glacier) or choosing compute types (Serverless vs. Containers) based on cost/predictability.

Starting from the basics with **11.5 years of experience** is actually an advantage. You already have the "tribal knowledge" of what breaks in production; you just need to learn the formal vocabulary and the "Big Tech" patterns to describe those failures.

System Design is less about "memorizing" and more about **building a toolbox**. Here is the most efficient path to go from zero to "Senior SDE Ready."

---

## Phase 1: The Foundations (The "Building Blocks")

Before you design a system, you must understand the components. Don't rush this.

* **The "Rule of Three":** Understand that every system is a trade-off between **Consistency, Availability, and Partition Tolerance (CAP Theorem).** You can't have all three perfectly at scale.
* **Networking Basics:** Refresh on DNS, Load Balancers (L4 vs. L7), and the difference between TCP and UDP.
* **The Power of Hashing:** Learn how **Consistent Hashing** allows us to distribute data across 1,000 servers without breaking everything when one server dies.

## Phase 2: The Data Tier (Where the "Truth" Lives)

In Senior interviews, the database choice is usually where candidates pass or fail.

* **Storage Engines:** Understand why you'd use a **Relational DB** (PostgreSQL/MySQL) for a bank and a **NoSQL DB** (Cassandra/DynamoDB) for a social media feed.
* **Caching:** Learn the "Cache Aside" vs. "Write-Through" patterns.
* **Indexes:** Understand how a database actually finds data. If you don't know what a **B-Tree** or an **Inverted Index** is, start there.

## Phase 3: The "Senior" Nuances (The "L5" Level)

This is what separates a coder from a Senior Engineer.

* **Idempotency:** How to ensure an operation (like a payment) only happens once, even if the "Submit" button is clicked five times.
* **Rate Limiting:** How to protect your servers from being overwhelmed by a single "noisy" user.
* **Microservices Communication:** When to use **REST/gRPC** (Synchronous) vs. **Message Queues/Kafka** (Asynchronous).

## Your "Baseline" Study Plan

* Week 1: Core Concepts & Load Balancing
Master the CAP Theorem, Vertical vs. Horizontal Scaling, and how Load Balancers distribute traffic.


* Week 2: Databases & Caching
Study SQL vs NoSQL, Sharding, Replication, and Redis caching strategies.


* Week 3: Asynchronous Systems
Learn about Message Queues (Kafka/RabbitMQ) and why decoupling services is mandatory for scale.


* Week 4: The 'Standard' Designs
Apply your knowledge to classic problems: "Design a URL Shortener" or "Design a Top-K Leaderboard."
